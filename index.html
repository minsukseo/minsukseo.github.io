I design atoms for the physical world.

Hands-on thin-film materials engineer with expertise in sputter deposition, plasma/radiation-assisted processing, failure analysis, and scale-up of 2D-3D materials and high-entropy alloys from R&D to prototype production. Proven track record of leading high-impact projects at LLNL and top research universities (UIUC, Stanford, Penn State) on advanced materials for semiconductors, nuclear fusion, and energy storage.

Postdoctoral Researcher in Materials Science and Engineering at UIUC, formerly at Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory. Ph.D. in Nuclear Engineering from Penn State University.

Projects

High Entropy Alloy Design and Metastable Structures
UIUC, Materials Science and Engineering Department, 2026 - Present
Sputter synthesis of multi-element Ni-based alloys and characterization of ion-irradiation-induced microstructural modifications (PI: Prof. Marie Charpagne). Routine site-specific FIB preparation (Scios 2, Helios) and advanced TEM operation (Talos F200X G2) for defect analysis.
NIF target fabrication, LLNL, 2025
Coauthored work (PI: Dr. Sergei Kucheyev) with Blue Laser Fusion Inc. (Prof. Shuji Nakamura) on advanced material integration for fusion energy, focusing on PVD sputter deposition of boron carbide and rare-earth high-entropy alloy thin films.
Radiation Defect Engineering in Two-Dimensional Membranes
LLNL, Materials Science Division, 2022 - 2025
Conducted LDRD project in collaboration with Prof. Steven Chu (1997 Nobel Laureate) at Stanford on 2D Li-battery materials. Spearheaded wafer-scale thin film growth of h-BN (~100 batches, >1,000 samples, 10 nm–1 µm). Optimized magnetron sputter deposition processes (temperature, gas pressure, bias voltage) alongside ion bombardment using an ion accelerator [paper 1] [paper 2] to systematically tune structural characteristics, successfully reducing crystallinity metrics from 100 down to 30.
Nuclear Fusion Materials in Extreme Environments
Penn State, Nuclear Engineering Department, 2022
Investigated microstructural evolution, surface oxidation, and nanomechanical degradation of tungsten-based materials under high heat flux and extreme plasma-material interaction environments (PI: Prof. Leigh Winfrey).

STEM DF cross-sectional microstructure (200 kV, 7.26 kx) relevant to Radiation Defect Engineering.
