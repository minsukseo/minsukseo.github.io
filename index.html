I design atoms for the physical world.

Hands-on thin-film materials engineer with expertise in sputter deposition, plasma/radiation-assisted processing, failure analysis, and scale-up of 2D-3D materials and high-entropy alloys from R&D to prototype production. Proven track record of leading high-impact projects at a national laboratory (LLNL) and top research universities (UIUC, Stanford, Penn State) on advanced materials for deep tech, including semiconductors, nuclear fusion, and energy technology.

I am currently a Postdoctoral Researcher in Materials Science and Engineering at the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign, formerly with Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory, where I collaborated with group of Prof. Steven Chu (1997 Nobel Laureate in Physics) at Stanford University. I hold a Ph.D. in Nuclear Engineering from Penn State University.

Projects

High Entropy Alloy Design and Metastable Structures
University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign (UIUC), Materials Science and Engineering Department, 2026 - Present
Sputter synthesis of multi-element (ternary, quaternary, and quinary) Ni-based alloys and characterization of ion-irradiation-induced microstructural modifications (PI: Prof. Marie Charpagne).
Produce high-entropy alloy thin films via magnetron sputtering using an AJA Orion 8 Magnetron Sputtering System, conduct routine site-specific sample preparation using focused ion beam (FIB, Scios 2 and Helios DualBeam), and operate an advanced transmission electron microscope (TEM, Talos F200X G2) for in-depth microstructural and defect analysis.
National Ignition Facility (NIF) target fabrication, LLNL, 2025
Coauthored work (PI: Dr. Sergei Kucheyev) with researchers at Blue Laser Fusion Inc., founded by Prof. Shuji Nakamura (2014 Nobel Laureate in Physics), focusing on advanced material integration for fusion energy systems. Contributed to the PVD sputter deposition of boron carbide and rare-earth high-entropy alloy thin films, driving surface defect characterization and process optimization to enhance film quality and scalability.
Radiation Defect Engineering in Two-Dimensional Membranes
Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory (LLNL), Materials Science Division, 2022 - 2025
Conducted LDRD project in collaboration with the Chu group at Stanford on advanced two-dimensional Li-battery materials research. Spearheaded wafer-scale thin film growth of hexagonal boron nitride (h-BN), handling nearly 100 batches and over 1,000 samples spanning thicknesses from 10 nm to 1 µm. Optimized magnetron sputter deposition processes (temperature, gas pressure, and bias voltage) alongside ion bombardment using an ion accelerator to systematically tune structural characteristics, successfully reducing crystallinity metrics from 100 down to 30.
Nuclear Fusion Materials in Extreme Environments
Pennsylvania State University, Nuclear Engineering Department, 2022
Investigated microstructural evolution, surface oxidation, and nanomechanical degradation of tungsten-based materials under high heat flux and extreme plasma-material interaction environments (PI: Prof. Leigh Winfrey).

Tungsten plasma-facing material response under high heat flux conditions: Surface morphology deformation (left) paired with cross-sectional TEM microstructural analysis (right).
